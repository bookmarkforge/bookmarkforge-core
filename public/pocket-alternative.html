Pocket is going away. Smart search is included free — Raindrop charges yearly for it. BookmarkForge works on any device with no device cap on Free, while your reading list stays encrypted and yours.

Why the Shutdown Changes Everything

Pocket closing is not just a missing app — it is a reminder that a read-it-later service holding your list on someone else's servers can disappear overnight.

Your list is gone with it

Years of saved articles, tags and reading history lived on Pocket's servers. When the service goes away, so does that data — unless you export it first.

Local-first is the only guarantee

BookmarkForge stores your vault in your browser's local database. No server, no shutdown can take it away. Offline, encrypted, and always yours.

You keep the keys

Your data is encrypted with keys derived from your master password (AES-256-GCM + Argon2id). No account, no backdoor, no "forgot password" reset — just your keys.

Migrate from Pocket in 4 Steps

Your library — titles, tags and original save dates — moves to your device in minutes. Smart search is included free (Raindrop charges yearly for it), and Free works on any device with no device cap. No account, no upload.

  1. Export your Pocket library

    If you still have access to Pocket, use its export feature to download the HTML file of your saved list. The file contains every link with its title, tags and original save date.

  2. Open the BookmarkForge importer

    Open BookmarkForge (no account needed) and click Open Importer on the dashboard. The importer accepts .html, .json, .csv, .md and .zip files.

  3. Drop in your Pocket file

    BookmarkForge detects the Pocket HTML export format automatically and imports every link with its title, tags and original date. Items without tags are labeled pocket-import so you can find them instantly.

  4. Make it yours

    Search by meaning for free — Raindrop charges yearly for that feature. Free has no device cap, so use BookmarkForge on any device, then add notes, folders and private peer-to-peer sync. Everything stays encrypted on your devices.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I import my Pocket library into BookmarkForge?
Yes. Export your list from Pocket as an HTML file, then open BookmarkForge's importer and drop the file in. BookmarkForge detects the Pocket HTML export format automatically and imports every link with its title, tags and original save date. Untagged items are labeled "pocket-import" so you can find them instantly.
Does migrating from Pocket require an account?
No. BookmarkForge has no accounts. The import runs entirely on your device — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
Will my imported bookmarks be sent to a server?
No. BookmarkForge is local-first: your bookmarks, notes and documents are stored in your browser's local database and never leave your device unless you explicitly export, share or sync them.
What import formats does BookmarkForge support?
The importer accepts .html (including Pocket and Netscape bookmark exports), .json, .csv, .md and .zip (Markdown/Obsidian vault) files.
Is the comparison table still valid after Pocket closes?
The table reflects Pocket's feature set as it existed before the shutdown announcement. Once the service is fully retired, any Pocket-specific rows (export, offline app) become unavailable by definition — BookmarkForge's side of the table is unaffected because it runs locally.
